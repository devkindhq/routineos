How it works One loop, every time

It goes round. Each time, it gets a little better.

A routine is a job Claude does for you on a schedule, like checking your inbox every morning or watching for new job posts twice a week.
  1. 1
    Claude does the jobOn your schedule, like every morning at 7.
  2. 2
    You look at the DeskOne private page shows what it found, what it skipped, and why.
  3. 3
    You keep or drop what it foundKeep an email it skipped. Drop a post it kept. That is all the teaching it needs.
  4. 4
    It suggests a better settingWhen your choices point the same way, it suggests one small change and shows why.
  5. 5
    You say yes or noOr later. It never changes its own settings.
  6. 6
    Next time round, it is betterThe next run uses what you agreed to. Then the loop goes again.
You stay in charge

It asks. You decide.

Nothing goes out without your yes

Emails, posts and deletions wait on your Desk until you approve them.

The first run is a preview

It only shows you what it would do. You check it before it does anything.

The web is information, not orders

If a web page or an email says "do this", Claude reads it as information. It never follows it as an order.

It never changes its own settings

It can only suggest a change. You are the one who says yes.

Get started in Claude About two minutes

Add it to the Claude you already use.

Pick where you use Claude. Follow the steps. Then ask Claude for your first routine in plain words.

Free and open source. Runs on the Claude plan you already pay for.

  1. Open Claude and go to Customize > Connectors.
  2. Click + Add, then Add custom connector.
  3. Name it RoutineOS and paste this address. Choose No sign in, then Add.
    https://docs.routineos.org/api/mcp
  4. In a chat, turn it on from + > Connectors. Then ask:
    Set up the jobs watch template

Free plans allow one custom connector. On Team and Enterprise, an owner adds it under Organization settings > Connectors.

  1. Type this in Claude Code:
    /plugin install routineos --marketplace devkindhq/routineos
  2. Ask Claude, or run /schedule:
    Make me a routine that watches job posts twice a week

Needs Claude Code 2.1.275 or later.

The bigger loop From idea to retired

Every routine has a life, from first idea to switched off.

Eight stages. Each one ends with something you can check.

1 · optional

Discover

You agree, in writing, on what the job is.

2

Design

You approve the plan. The risks are listed.

3

Build

It is put together and checked. Every outside place it contacts is listed.

4

Verify

A first run that only shows you what it would do. You check it.

5

Deploy

It is switched on, on the schedule you chose.

6

Operate

Your Desk shows that it is healthy and what it costs.

7

Improve

A change you said yes to, checked and explained in plain words.

8

Retire

Switched off. Nothing left on a schedule. Saved passwords deleted.

Done means it is live and you have checked it.

Four routine types

new --type scaffolds serverless, watch, approval-gated or interactive.

Learning guardrails

  • Signals are counted by code, not guessed by the model.
  • At least 5 cases and a 60% share before a suggestion. At most 3 per run.
  • Never learns sending, gates, permissions or budgets. A rejection is remembered 14 days.

CLI

$ python3 skills/routineos/scripts/routineos.py doctor $ python3 skills/routineos/scripts/routineos.py new my-watch --type watch $ python3 skills/routineos/scripts/routineos.py lint my-watch

The linter fails on

  • a setting with no default
  • a schedule faster than hourly (cron minimum is one hour)
  • a prompt without the untrusted-data clause

Questions

Plain answers.

What is a routine?

A job Claude does for you on a schedule, like checking your inbox every morning or watching for new job posts twice a week. RoutineOS gives each routine a Desk and helps it get better each time.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. In Claude Desktop you add it in a few clicks, then ask for what you want in plain words. Claude sets it up and asks you to check the first run.

Will it send emails or post without asking?

No. Anything that sends, posts or deletes waits on your Desk for your yes. The first run only shows you what it would do.

How does it get better?

It watches what you keep and what you drop. When your choices point the same way often enough, it suggests one small change and shows why. You say yes, no or later. It never changes itself.

What does it cost?

RoutineOS is free and open source. Routines run on the Claude plan you already pay for.

How often can a routine run?

At most once an hour. Each run starts fresh. Scheduled jobs in Claude are still an early preview, and "Run now" on the Desk joins a queue rather than starting that second.
